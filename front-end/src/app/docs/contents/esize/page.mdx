# Single-Group Summary Transformations (Generic Inverse-Variance Preparation)

## 1. Dichotomous Data Preparation 
(for proportional / prevalence meta-analysis and generic inverse-variance pooling)

### A) Needed Values

To prepare single-arm dichotomous data into raw and logit-transformed summary statistics, you need the following values:

1) Number of events (`Events`)
2) Total number of participants (`Total`)

### B) Steps

1) In the input table, enter `Events` and `Total` into their corresponding cells.
2) Click **Submit** (or **Convert**).
3) In the output table, you will get the raw proportion ($p$), the logit-transformed proportion ($\text{logit}(p)$), and their corresponding standard errors ready for generic inverse-variance pooling.

### C) Equations

Outputs are calculated based on the following equations:

**1) Raw Proportion (Prevalence):**

$$\text{Prevalence Estimate } (p) = \frac{\text{Event}}{\text{Total}}$$

$$\text{SE}_{(p)} = \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{\text{Total}}}$$

**2) Logit-Transformed Proportion:**

$$\text{logit}(p) = \ln\left(\frac{p}{1 - p}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{\text{Event}}{\text{Total} - \text{Event}}\right)$$

**3) Standard Error (SE) [Logit-Transformed]:**

$$\text{SE}_{\text{logit}(p)} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\text{Event}} + \frac{1}{\text{Total} - \text{Event}}}$$

### D) Citations and Sources

1) Package ‘meta’ ([meta: General Package for Meta-Analysis](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/meta/meta.pdf)).
2) Barendregt JJ, Doi SA, Lee YY, Pacella R, Vos T. Meta-analysis of prevalence. *Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health*. 2013;67(11):974-8.
3) Barker TH, Migliavaca CB, Stein C, et al. Conducting proportional meta-analysis in different types of systematic reviews: a guide for synthesisers of evidence. *BMC Med Res Methodol*. 2021;21:189.
4) Higgins JPT, Thomas J, Chandler J, Cumpston M, Li T, Page MJ, Welch VA (editors). *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions* version 6.4 (updated August 2023). Cochrane; 2023. Chapter 6: Choosing effect measures and computing estimates of effect.

### E) Additional Notes

**A) Generic Inverse-Variance Integration:** In software packages (such as RevMan or R `meta::metagen`) that do not feature dedicated single-arm proportion models, users can pool single-arm studies using the generic inverse-variance method. The logit-transformed proportion is entered as the treatment effect (`TE` / `logTE`) and its standard error as `seTE` / `logSeTE`.

**B) Variance Stabilization:** For prevalence meta-analysis, pooling raw proportions directly can cause confidence limits to fall outside the 0 to 1 interval. Furthermore, the variance of proportions near 0 or 1 approaches zero, artificially inflating study weights. The logit transformation maps proportions to an unrestricted continuous scale ($-\infty$ to $+\infty$), stabilizing variance and enabling valid back-transformation.

**C) Zero-Event and All-Event Studies:** In boundary scenarios where a study reports zero events ($\text{Event} = 0$) or 100% events ($\text{Event} = \text{Total}$), an empirical continuity correction of $0.5$ is added to both the event and non-event counts. This prevents division by zero and infinite estimates ($\pm\infty$) while preserving study weighting.

## 2. Continuous Data Preparation 
(for single-arm continuous cohorts and generic inverse-variance pooling)

### A) Needed Values

To prepare continuous summary statistics from a single cohort, you need the following values:

1) Mean ($\bar{x}$)
2) Standard deviation (`SD`)
3) Total number of participants (`N`)

### B) Steps

1) In the input table, enter `Mean`, `SD`, and `N` into their corresponding cells.
2) Click **Submit** (or **Convert**).
3) In the output table, you will get the cohort Mean, its standard error ($\text{SE}$), the natural logarithm of the mean ($\ln(\text{Mean})$), and its log standard error.

### C) Equations

Outputs are calculated based on standard continuous summary statistics:

**1) Sample Mean:**

$$\text{Mean} = \bar{x}$$

**2) Standard Error (SE) [Raw Mean]:**

$$\text{SE} = \frac{\text{SD}}{\sqrt{N}}$$

**3) Log-Transformed Mean:**

$$\ln(\text{Mean}) = \ln(\bar{x})$$

$$\text{SE}_{\ln(\text{Mean})} = \frac{\text{SD}}{\bar{x}\sqrt{N}}$$

### D) Citations and Sources

1) Higgins JPT, Thomas J, Chandler J, Cumpston M, Li T, Page MJ, Welch VA (editors). *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions* version 6.5 (updated August 2023). Cochrane; 2023. Chapter 6: Continuous outcome data.
2) Package ‘meta’ ([meta: General Package for Meta-Analysis](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/meta/meta.pdf)).

### E) Additional Notes

**A) Generic Inverse-Variance Integration:** When single-arm continuous data must be entered into generic inverse-variance modules (such as RevMan or R `meta::metagen`), the cohort Mean is entered as `TE` and its standard error ($\text{SD}/\sqrt{N}$) is entered as `seTE`.

**B) Terminology Clarification:** A single cohort summary provides a parameter estimate (sample mean), not a mean difference. The term "Mean Difference" is strictly reserved for two-group comparative analyses or pre-post baseline change comparisons where an explicit contrast is computed.
